Skystack: Aurora Privacy Policy

Last updated: [set at publication] · Applies to the Skystack: Aurora app for iOS and Android

The short version. Skystack: Aurora is a small arcade game. It has no accounts or servers of ours: your settings, scores and streak are kept on your device, and in your phone's backup if you have it turned on. Starting with version 1.0 on Android, Google Play Games adds Google leaderboards and achievements. If you already have an active Play Games session, the game can use it. You can choose to sign in from the game’s Settings. Google holds Play Games data (section 12). Starting with version 1.0 on iPhone and iPad, Apple's Game Center adds leaderboards and achievements. If you are already signed in to Game Center on your device, the game uses that; otherwise you can sign in from the game's Settings, or not at all. Apple holds Game Center data (section 13). The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. Google's ad software, its consent message, Google Play Games on Android and Game Center on iPhone and iPad are the only parts of the app that process personal data. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland the app asks for your consent before the first ad. One in-app purchase removes the ads; the store handles the payment and we never see your card.

2. What the app itself stores

The app keeps the following on your device, in its own private storage. We cannot read it, and uninstalling the app deletes it from the device:

  • Your settings: sound, haptics, theme and language, and, in versions that offer them, reminders, intensity (how strong the game's effects are), reduce motion, color-blind friendly and high contrast. A choice made in one version stays stored on your device even if a later version no longer uses it.
  • Your best score, your streak and the day it was last extended, how many rounds you have played and when the recent ones ended, when you installed the app, whether you have seen the tutorial, and when you last tapped the prompt to rate the app. The round and install times exist only so the app can keep ads sparse (no ad right after you install, after a very short round, or after a personal best).
  • Temporary sound files the app generates in its cache. They contain no information about you.
  • Whether the "Remove ads" purchase is owned, so the app knows not to show ads.

Your phone's backup. If your phone's own backup is turned on (on Android, the backup to your Google account; on iPhone and iPad, iCloud Backup or a backup to your computer), it can include a copy of what the app keeps in its private storage: the data above, apart from the temporary sound files, and what the consent message and the ads SDK keep there, such as your consent choice. The copy is kept in your Google or Apple account or on your computer. Uninstalling the app does not delete it: it stays until the backup is deleted or replaced. If you reinstall the app on Android, restore a backup, or copy your apps and data to a new phone, your settings, scores and streak can come back. We have no access to the backup. You can turn it off or delete it in your phone's backup settings, or on your computer for a computer backup. The "Remove ads" purchase comes back through "Restore purchases" either way (section 5).

If you use "Share", the app draws a picture of your tower, with the game's name and a link to get it, and hands it with a line of text (your height, the game's name and the link) to your phone's own share sheet. What happens next is up to the app you pick; nothing is sent to us.

Reminders to play are off until you turn them on. When they are on, the app schedules them on your device, and they appear as ordinary notifications. Nothing about them is sent to us or to anyone else. Your choice is saved on your device as one of your settings.

3. Advertising (Google AdMob)

Unless you have bought "Remove ads", the app shows an interstitial ad after some rounds and an optional rewarded ad when you choose "Second chance". These ads are served by Google AdMob through the Google Mobile Ads SDK, which is software from Google LLC that runs inside the app.

To serve and measure ads, the SDK may collect and send to Google:

  • Your device's advertising identifier (Google Advertising ID on Android, IDFA on iOS, only if you allow tracking), and other device and app information such as model, operating system, language, time zone and the app's name and version.
  • An identifier shared by one developer's apps on a device: the app set ID on Android, given by Google Play services, and the identifier for vendor (IDFV) on iOS, given by Apple. Unlike the advertising ID, neither has a reset in the settings; they change when you uninstall the last of our apps or factory-reset the phone.
  • Your IP address, from which an approximate location may be derived.
  • How you use the app and its ads: app launches, taps, that an ad was shown, viewed or tapped, video views, and which rewarded ad you completed.
  • Diagnostic information about how the app and the SDK perform, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy use.

Google uses this data for advertising (showing ads and, where you have consented or allowed tracking, personalizing them), for analytics (measuring ads and how the app and the SDK perform) and to prevent fraud. Google acts as an independent controller for that processing. How Google handles the data is described in the Google Privacy Policy and in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services. Google is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework for transfers outside the EEA.

Legal basis (EEA, UK, Switzerland). Personalized ads and the storing or reading of identifiers on your device happen only with your consent (GDPR Article 6(1)(a), ePrivacy rules); you can withdraw it at any time as described in section 4, which does not affect what was done before. Showing non-personalized ads, measuring them and preventing fraud rely on Google's and our legitimate interest in funding a free app and keeping ads safe (Article 6(1)(f)); you can object through the same choices. The consent message's own processing (section 4) relies on the obligation to obtain and be able to show your consent (Articles 6(1)(c) and 7(1)) and, for working out which rules apply where you are, on the legitimate interest in showing you the right message (Article 6(1)(f)). We make no decisions about you by automated means that have legal or similarly significant effects.

Google may work with the ad technology providers it lists at Ad technology providers; the consent message in the app names the ones active for Skystack. We do not place any other ad network, tracker or analytics service in the app.

4. Consent and your choices

EEA, UK and Switzerland. Before the first ad, the app shows a consent message (Google's User Messaging Platform). You can accept, or choose "Manage options" to decide purpose by purpose, including objecting to processing based on legitimate interest. If you do not consent to personalized ads, Google serves ads that are not based on your personal data. You can change your choice at any time from the app's Settings, under "Privacy options".

The consent message itself. The consent message is Google software that runs inside the app. To know which consent rules apply where you are and to show the right message, it uses your IP address, from which Google estimates your approximate region, together with information about the app and your device. It also records how the message performs and what you choose in it. Google uses this data to run the consent message, and Google declares that it does not link this data to your identity or use it for advertising. Your choice is stored on your device so the app can apply it (section 2).

iOS. On iPhone and iPad the app also shows Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt. If you choose "Ask App Not to Track", the SDK does not read your IDFA. You can change this later in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

Android. You can reset or delete your advertising ID and opt out of ad personalization in Android Settings → Google → Ads.

US states. Where a state privacy law gives you the right to opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of personal data, the app shows a "Do not sell or share my personal information" choice through the same consent tooling.

No ads at all. Buying "Remove ads" turns the ads off, and with them the data the ads SDK collects. The consent message still runs at launch to check which rules apply where you are (above).

Reminders. To stop them, turn off "Reminders" in the app's Settings, or turn off the app's notifications in your phone's settings.

5. The in-app purchase

"Remove ads" is a one-time purchase made through the Apple App Store or Google Play. The store processes the payment and holds your payment details; the store never gives us your card or bank details. The store tells the app only that the purchase is owned, and the app remembers that on your device. "Restore purchases" asks the store again, for a new phone or a reinstall. Receipts come from the store. Refunds follow the store's rules and our Terms of Service: on Google Play we can also refund a purchase ourselves from the store's order record, which doesn't include your payment details. Only Apple can refund an App Store purchase, so where the Terms give you money back and Apple doesn't refund it, we pay you directly: what you send us for that (email, order number, bank details) is used only to pay you, shared only with our bank and accountant, and kept only as long as accounting law requires (GDPR Article 6(1)(b) and (c)).

6. What we do not do

  • No account of ours, and no email address or real name is asked for or stored. The only sign-ins are Google Play Games on Android and Game Center on iPhone and iPad, and nothing in the game requires them (sections 12 and 13).
  • No analytics or crash-reporting service of ours runs in the app. Apple and Google may share aggregated crash and usage statistics with us only if you have opted in to that in your device settings.
  • No data is sold by us for money, and we hold no data of yours to sell. Under some US state laws, letting Google show you personalized ads can count as "sharing" personal data for cross-context behavioral advertising; section 11 explains the opt-out.
  • No precise location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera access.

7. Children

Skystack is not directed at children under 13 (or the age of digital consent where you live), and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. Ad requests are not flagged as child-directed.

If you think a child has used the app, here is what you should know. The app itself keeps no personal data; what it stores is kept on the device, and in the phone's backup if that is turned on (section 2). Google's ad software, its consent message and Google Play Games send data to Google under the Google Privacy Policy (sections 3, 4 and 12). That data does not go to servers of ours. On iPhone and iPad, Game Center sends data to Apple under the Apple Privacy Policy (section 13), and that data does not go to servers of ours either. If Apple marks a Game Center account as underage, the game does not use Game Center for it. Use Google’s controls to delete the data Google holds. Where the app shows it, Settings → "Privacy options" changes the consent given for ads, and section 4 lists the other ad choices on the device; none of them deletes data already sent. For the data Google holds, use Google's My Ad Center and Google's privacy controls; for Google Play Games data, Google's Delete your Play Games data or Play Games profile; for Game Center data, Apple's Data and Privacy page. If you need help with any of these, write to us at the email address in section 1.

8. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or receive the personal data about you, to object to or restrict its processing, to withdraw consent, and to complain to a supervisory authority: in Romania the ANSPDCP, in the UK the ICO, or the authority where you live. Because we never receive the data the app stores, the practical ways to exercise these rights are: uninstall the app or clear its storage to delete that data from your device, and use your phone's backup settings to delete any copy in its backup (section 2); use the consent and tracking choices in section 4 for advertising; and for data Google holds, use Google's My Ad Center and Google's privacy controls, and for Google Play Games data, Google Play Games' own settings (section 12); and for Game Center data, Apple's Data and Privacy page (section 13). For anything else, write to us at the address in section 1; we answer within one month, and we will never treat you differently for exercising a right.

9. Retention and security

Data stored by the app stays on your device until you clear the app's storage (Android) or uninstall the app. A copy in your phone's backup stays until that backup is deleted or replaced (section 2). Google's retention of advertising data and of Google Play Games data is described in Google's privacy policy. Apple's retention of Game Center data is described in Apple's privacy policy (section 13). The app talks to Google's servers only over encrypted connections.

12. Google Play Games (Android)

Starting with version 1.0 on Android, the game uses Google Play Games for Google leaderboards and achievements. If the app shows a consent message at launch, that comes first. On an update that shows the privacy notice, the game checks for an active Play Games session after you dismiss the notice. Without an active session, you can start a round right away or choose Sign in from the game’s Settings. You can play and earn in-game achievements without signing in. Google leaderboards and Google achievement screens require sign-in.

  • What the game gets. The game checks whether you are signed in. When restoring your best height, it reads your score from Google. Google’s own screens can show your profile according to your Play Games settings. The game does not store your gamertag, avatar or player ID or give them to the ad software in section 3.
  • What goes to Google. When you are signed in, the app sends your scores and achievements to Google. Google Play Games also collects analytics and diagnostics for its service. Google runs Google Play Games, acts as an independent controller of this data, and holds it under the Google Privacy Policy. We never receive it on servers of ours.
  • How we use it. The app uses Play Games to show Google leaderboards and achievements. After a reinstall, it can restore your best height when a score is available on the signed-in profile’s Best height leaderboard. It keeps any higher best height already on the device. We never use Play Games data for advertising.
  • Legal basis (EEA, UK, Switzerland). Our legitimate interest in offering leaderboards and achievements (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). You can object at any time by signing out of Google Play Games, as below, or by writing to us (section 1).
  • Your choices. You can play without signing in. Your Play Games profile settings decide who sees your profile and game activity, and let you choose an account for an individual game or sign out of it (Google's Play Games profile privacy & other settings). To delete Skystack's Play Games data or your whole profile, follow Google's Delete your Play Games data or Play Games profile.

13. Game Center (iPhone and iPad)

Starting with version 1.0 on iPhone and iPad, the game uses Apple's Game Center for leaderboards and achievements. If the app shows a consent message or an update notice when it starts, that comes first. Then, if you are already signed in to Game Center on your device, the game uses that. The game never shows Game Center's sign-in when it starts: you can sign in from the Game Center row in the game's Settings, or not at all. You can play and earn in-game achievements without Game Center. Game Center leaderboards and achievement screens require sign-in. If Apple marks your Game Center account as underage, the game does not use Game Center: it sends no scores or achievements, reads no score, and shows no Game Center controls, not even the Game Center row in Settings.

  • What the game gets. The game checks whether you are signed in, and whether Apple marks your account as underage; it does not keep or log that mark. When restoring your best height, it reads only your own score from Apple. The game does not read your Game Center nickname, avatar or player identifiers.
  • What goes to Apple. When you are signed in, the game sends Apple the height and the longest perfect chain of each finished round, for its two leaderboards, and the achievements you earn, with your progress on the ones that build up over time. Achievements and best scores you reached before signing in are sent when you sign in. Apple runs Game Center, acts as an independent controller of this data, and holds it under the Apple Privacy Policy. What Apple collects on its own, and what other Game Center players can see, is described in Apple's Game Center & Privacy. We never receive it on servers of ours.
  • How we use it. The game uses Game Center to show Apple's leaderboards and achievements. The game also has one Game Center challenge, on Best height, which runs in Apple's own screens; the game sends nothing for it beyond the scores above. After a reinstall, it can restore your best height when a score is available on your own Best height leaderboard entry. It keeps any higher best height already on the device. We never use Game Center data for advertising, and none of it goes to the ad software or the consent message in sections 3 and 4.
  • Legal basis (EEA, UK, Switzerland). Our legitimate interest in offering leaderboards and achievements (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). You can object at any time by signing out of Game Center, as below, or by writing to us (section 1).
  • Your choices. You can play without Game Center. To sign out, or to change your Game Center profile, go to iOS Settings → Game Center. To get a copy of the data Apple holds, or to delete it, use Apple's Data and Privacy page.
